### De novo transcriptome assembly allows identification (discovery) and classification of non-coding RNAs in whitefish exposed to MC-LR
Here, based on the procedure for identifying non-coding RNAs in non-model species first described by Harris et al., we managed to successfully separate protein coding transcripts and further discover evolutionary conserved non-coding RNAs and putative novel non-coding transcripts in Coregonus lavaterus. As whitefish does not have annotated genome, based on de novo assembled genome built from liver transcriptome we binned them into three non-overlapping groups. First contained non-coding RNAs that showed homology to sequences deposited in Rfam database (known group). Second contained contigs larger than 200 nucleotides with no homology to any database we have tested and we labeled them as putative novel long non-coding RNAs (84,974 contigs). During process of filtering of transcripts we have noticed, that a large portion of non-coding contigs has homology to untranslated regions of mRNAs present in RefSeq database (104,024 contigs). These contigs were further paired with corresponding protein coding contigs and its relationship were analyzed (UTR group). Our overall approach with numbers of particular transcripts is summarized in the workflow diagram (Figure 1). Knowing that our results based on single de novo assembled transcriptome could have high level of redundancy, our further analysis were based only on contigs that expression were impaired at least 2-fold with P adjusted value (lower) 0.001 after MC-LR exposure.


### Changes in expression of evolutionary conserved non-coding RNAs include long non-coding RNAs
The list of transcripts, that were depleted of protein coding transcripts as well as those which showed any coding potential (as verified by SVM-based Coding Potential Calculator) were compared with non-coding transcripts deposited in Rfam database. This produced the list of xxx contigs (known NCT), which were counted among experimental groups and differential expression analysis were conducted (Figure 2a). Among 4,238 differentially expressed evolutionary conserved non-coding RNAs, there were 94 known long non-coding RNAs identified (Figure 2b). 


### Comparison between coding and putative novel long non-coding transcripts reveals differences in MFE, length and content of GC base pairs
To determine if the identified putative whitefish lncRNAs candidates differ from PCT in terms of a higher free-energy level than the protein coding mRNAs, we used the RNAfold algorithm from the ViennaRNA package. It has been previously observed that the secondary structure of lncRNAs tend to have higher free energy (less stable conformation) than protein-coding mRNAs [12, 44, 45]. Obtained free energy values of secondary structures were corrected for the length of the sequence (Figure 3 A). The mean length-corrected minimum free energy for annotated protein coding genes was −0.289 kcal/mol/nt with a standard deviation of 0.059464 kcal/mol/nt. The mean length-corrected minimum free energy content of the putative lncRNAs was −0.237 kcal/mol/nt with a standard deviation of 0.038758 kcal/mol/nt. The mean length-corrected minimum free energy content of the putative lncRNAs was 1.22 kcal/mol/nt higher than that of the protein-coding transcripts (t() = 46,652, p (lower) 0.001, [0.04963723, 0.05399174]). Our data is consistent with previous reports showing that also whitefish lncRNAs fold into secondary structures of lower free energy than protein-coding transcripts. Moreover the mean content of GC base pairs was higher in protein-coding transcripts (t() = 56,164, p (lower) 0.001, [0.06915164, 0.06448719]) (Figure 3 B). Additionally length of the transcripts was found to be different in both groups (Figure 3 C). In summary, our comparison analysis showed structural differences between putative lncRNAs and PCT, validating our adopted methodology for discovery of lncRNAs in whitefish.


### MC-LR alters expression profiles of putative novel lncRNAs identified in this study
To investigate whether MC-LR induces changes in expression profiles of potent novel lncRNAs discovered in this study we compared expression profiles of control samples with samples exposed to MC-LR in three time steps (1d, 6d, 9d) using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor. Using a P adjusted (lower than) 0.001 and a 2-fold expression as a cutoff we identified 1739 and 2690 transcripts that were up- and down-regulated respectively. Transcripts of putative lncRNAs common between days of exposure shows that lncRNAs transcripts with altered expression profiles overlap with different stages of time-dependent toxicity induced by MC-LR (Figure X, A,B) [Woźny 2016]. This pattern is also true for protein coding transcripts identified in this study (Figure X, C,D). Days 6 and 9 shares more differentially expressed lncRNAs and PCT with each other than both combined share with day 1. This indicates, that also lncRNAs could distinguish acute (day 1) and chronic (day 6 and 9) phases of of MC-LR-induced toxicity, showing that identified lncRNAs could be decent predictors of MC-LR-induced toxicity in whitefish.


### Coexpression of autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated protein coding sequences (CDSs) after MC-LR exposure 
To investigate effects of MC-LR exposure on expression of non-coding contigs identified as autonomous 3'-UTRs, first we set them together with transcripts identified as coding mRNAs (PCT), that 3'-UTR is part of. Then, using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor we separately calculated differential expression of putative autonomous 3'-UTRs and corresponding PCT. Finally we compared percentages of corresponding contigs, that both were up- or down-regulated, as well as those which expression profiles were contradictory (Figure X). We found that, in the majority of cases, if an autonomous 3’-UTR was differentially expressed, the associated CDS was also differentially expressed in the same direction [is this the best way to say this?]. More than 80% of the 3’UTRs and their associatd CDSs were upregulated on day 1, and in about 10% of the cases, they were both downregulated. On days 6 and 9, a somewhat smaller percentage of 3’UTR/CDS pairs were differentially expressed in the same direction (about 80% on both days), and about 40% or more of the pairs were downregulated. The pattern of changes in expression of 3’UTR/CDS pairs was similar to the overall pattern of changes in expression of all CDS transcripts (paired with 3’UTRs and unpaired). When many of the paired CDSs were were upregulated, all CDSs tended to be upregulated, and vice-versa.

3’UTR/CDS pairs did something similar to all CDS transcripts (paired and unpaired)

We found that the majority of putative 3'-UTR non-coding contigs showed common coexpression with PCT (the same direction of expression change). It should be noted that proportions of commonly coexpressed contigs were different during advancing toxicity of MC-LR. The biggest disproportion of commonly coexpressed contigs could be seen at day 1, where over 80 % of all contigs in 3'-UTR/PCT group were up-regulated. In contrast, coexpression changes on days 6 and 9 were not as highly skewed and were similar to each other, again showing usefulness of non-coding transcripts in differentiating stages of MC-LR-induced toxicity progression [for discussion?]. Changes in coexpression 3'UTR/PCT pairs were in line with expression profiles of all differentially expressed PCT identified in this study with 1,467 contigs up- and only 315 down-regulated after the 1st day. 3,648 and 6,543 after 6th day, 1,766 and 903 after 9th day were up- and down-regulated respectively (Figure X, C,D).


### MC-LR antagonizes expression profiles of some autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated mRNAs 
As shown on figure X, we found that after MC-LR treatment not all expression profiles of pairs of 3'-UTR/PCT change in the same direction. In fact some of them showed the opposite direction in change of the expression. Day 1 showed the least number of this kind of contigs, but the proportions of the both groups with the opposite expression profiles remained stable throughout the experiment.
