### Results

#### Sequencing results
The number of raw reads in samples ranged from 50797688 to 70885836. Effective rate ranged from 95.88 to 99.25% ([Clean reads/Raw reads]*100%), with stable base error rate at 0.03% in all samples. Content of GC base pairs ranged form 47.23 to 50.50%. The detail statistics for the quality of sequencing data for each sample are presented in Supplementary Table 1.


#### Assessing quality of assembled *de novo* liver transcriptome 
The number of detected transcripts in our raw *de novo* assembled liver transcriptome was 1,136,890 with an average length of 367 base pairs. After the assembly, we mapped the fraction of trimmed reads back to the assembled liver transcriptome. The fraction of aligned reads was between 97 and 98% per sample. The final assembly, which was used in further analyses, was obtained by filtering too short, redundant and lowly expressed transcripts. The number of transcripts in our final assembly was 420,280 transcripts with an average length of 594 base pairs. The detail statistics for the final assembly are presented in the Supplementary Table 2.

BUSCO analysis pipeline revealed that of the 3640 Actinopterygian single-copy orthologs searched, our final assembly recovered 74.9% completely and 7.5% partially (Table 1). 17.6% of the single-copy orthologs were reported missing from our liver transcriptome.


#### *De novo* transcriptome assembly allowed discovery and classification of non-coding RNAs in whitefish exposed to MC-LR
Based on the procedure for identifying non-coding RNAs in non-model species first described by Harris et al., we first managed to separate protein coding transcripts (PCTs 148,646 contigs) and further to discover various long non-coding transcripts in whitefish (209,270) [@Harris2017]. Subsequent filtering steps allowed us to bin them into three non-overlapping groups. First group contained long non-coding RNAs that showed homology to sequences deposited in Rfam database (the group of known long non-coding transcripts with 20,272 contigs). Second group contained long non-coding transcripts that had homology with non-coding 3' untranslated regions of mRNA sequences deposited in RefSeq database and had associated protein coding transcript (autonomous 3'UTR/PCT group with 104,024 contigs). The third group contained long non-coding transcripts with no homology to any tested database (putative novel long non-coding RNAs with 84,974 contigs). Our filtering process is summarized in the workflow diagram (Figure 1). 


#### MC-LR exposure altered expression of evolutionary conserved lncRNAs
To obtain the list of evolutionary conserved long non-coding transcripts, transcripts left after removing protein coding transcripts (PCTs) were additionally checked for coding potential by SVM-based Coding Potential Calculator. Non-coding transcripts were further compared with sequences of transcripts deposited in the Rfam database. This produced the list of 20,272 contigs, which were counted and analyzed for differential expression (Figure 2a). Among 4,238 differentially expressed evolutionary conserved non-coding RNAs, there were 94 known, putatively conserved DE long non-coding RNAs identified, including MALAT1, HOTTIP, HOTAIR and HULC (Figure 2b). To show similarities in sequence conservation between whitefish and 17 other species, including human, we aligned sequence of our putative MALAT1 transcript with seed sequences of MALAT1 deposited in Rfam database (Supplementary Figure 2).


#### Coexpression of autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated PCTs after MC-LR exposure
To investigate effects of MC-LR exposure on expression of non-coding contigs identified as autonomous 3'-UTRs, first we set them together with transcripts identified as coding transcripts (PCTs), that 3'-UTR is part of. Then, using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor we separately calculated differential expression of putative autonomous 3'-UTRs and associated PCTs. Finally we compared percentages of corresponding contigs, that both were up- or down-regulated, as well as those which expression profiles were contradictory (Figure 3a). We found that, in the majority of cases, if an putative autonomous 3'-UTR was differentially expressed, the associated PCT was also differentially expressed in the same direction. The biggest shift in the direction of expression change was present after 1d of the experiment where over 82% of transcripts pairs were upregulated and only 11% downregulated. This separates d1 from both 6d and 9d time points where up- and down-regulated pairs were present about in the same proportions (around 40%). Moreover we checked whether the pattern of changes in expression of coexpressed 3'-UTR/PCT pairs was reflected in the pattern of changes in expression of all PCT (paired with 3’UTRs and unpaired). We found that at 6d and 9d time points both expression patterns were similar but not identical, however the ratio of up- and downregulated transcripts at 1d totaled 66 and 44% accordingly (Figure 3c,d). 

Gene Ontology analysis indicated similarities in GO terms of pairs that were simultaneously upregulated and downregulated after 6 and 9d of MC-LR exposure (Figure 4a,b). On the other hand, upregulated pairs at 1d were enriched in transcription regulator activity and DNA-binding transcription factor activity transcripts when compared with upregulated pairs at 6 and 9d (Figure 4c). Moreover, downregulated pairs at 1d were depleted in transcripts involved in enzyme regulator activity processes when compared with downregulated pairs at 6 and 9d of exposure.


#### MC-LR produced opposing profiles of some autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated PCTs
In addition, we found that MC-LR exposure changed expression of some 3'-UTRs in the opposite direction than PCTs of the same mRNA (Figure 3a). The lowest proportion of this type of transcripts (7%) was observed after 1d of MC-LR exposure, but it increased at 6 and 9d (19% and 16% accordingly). On the other hand, the proportions of both groups with the opposite expression profiles remained on the similar level throughout all days of exposure. GO terms analysis again showed similarities between 6 and 9d, whereas at 1d the transcripts were enriched in terms like 'signalling' or 'response to stimulus' in 3' UTRs down- and PCTs upregulated group. On the other hand the enriched transcripts at 6 and 9d were involved in 'cell', 'cell part', 'membrane' and 'membrane part' processes in 3' UTRs up- and PTCs downregulated group.


#### Comparison between putative novel lncRNAs and PCTs revealed differences in MFE, content of GC base pairs and length
To determine if the identified novel whitefish lncRNAs candidates differ from PCT in terms of a higher minimum free-energy (MFE) level than the protein coding transcripts, we used the RNAfold algorithm from the ViennaRNA package. Obtained free energy values of secondary structures were corrected for the length of the sequence (Figure 5a). The mean length-corrected minimum free energy of the putative lncRNAs was significantly higher than that of the annotated protein coding transcripts (-0.237 kcal/mol/nt -+ 0.038758 vs. -0.289 kcal/mol/nt -+ 0.059464 kcal/mol/nt, respectively, t() = 46.652, p (lower) 0.001, 95 % [0.04963723, 0.05399174]. The mean length-corrected MFE content of the putative lncRNAs was 1.22 kcal/mol/nt higher than that of the protein-coding transcripts. Moreover the mean content of GC base pairs was higher in protein-coding transcripts (t() = 56,164, p (lower) 0.001, [0.06915164, 0.06448719]) (Figure 5b). Finally length of the transcripts was found to be different in both groups (Figure 5c). In summary, comparison analysis showed structural differences between putative lncRNAs and annotated PCT, validating our adopted methodology for discovery of lncRNAs in whitefish.


#### MC-LR altered expression profiles of putative novel lncRNAs identified in this study
Using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor we investigated whether MC-LR induced changes in expression profiles of putative novel lncRNAs discovered in this study. Using a P adjusted (lower than) 0.001 and a 2-fold expression as a cutoff we identified 1739 and 2690 transcripts that were either up- or down-regulated between days of exposure, respectively. Figure 6 shows Venn diagrams with number of transcripts specific to the each time period, as well as those which overlap between days of exposure to MC-LR (Figure 6a,b). 31% of all up- and 34.8% of all down-regulated lncRNA transcripts were common between days 6 and 9 showing similar expression changes between these time periods. On the other hand, the similarities of common transcripts between day 1 and days 6 and 9 range from 0.23% to 5.31%. This pattern could be also observed in the protein coding transcripts identified in this study (Figure 3c,d).
