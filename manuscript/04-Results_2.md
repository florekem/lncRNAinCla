### De novo transcriptome assembly allows identification (discovery) and classification of non-coding RNAs in whitefish exposed to MC-LR
Based on the procedure for identifying non-coding RNAs in non-model species first described by Harris et al., we first managed to separate protein coding transcripts (PCTs, xxx contigs) and further to discover various non-coding transcripts in whitefish (188,998 + known). Subsequent filtering steps allowed us to bin them into three non-overlapping groups. First group contained non-coding RNAs that showed homology to sequences deposited in Rfam database (the group of known non-coding transcripts, known NCTs with xxx contigs). Second group contained non-coding transcripts that had homology with non-coding 3' untranslated regions of mRNA sequences deposited in RefSeq database and had associated protein coding transcript (autonomous 3'UTR/PCT group with 104,024 contigs). The third group contained non-coding transcripts larger than 200 nucleotides with no homology to any tested database (putative novel long non-coding RNAs with 84,974 contigs). Our filtering process, including the number of transcripts at each stage is summarized in the workflow diagram (Figure 1).

### Coexpression of autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated PCTs after MC-LR exposure
To investigate effects of MC-LR exposure on expression of non-coding contigs identified as autonomous 3'-UTRs, first we set them together with transcripts identified as coding transcripts (PCTs), that 3'-UTR is part of. Then, using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor we separately calculated differential expression of putative autonomous 3'-UTRs and associated PCTs. Finally we compared percentages of corresponding contigs, that both were up- or down-regulated, as well as those which expression profiles were contradictory (Figure X). We found that, in the majority of cases, if an autonomous 3'-UTR was differentially expressed, the associated PCTs was also differentially expressed in the same direction. More than 80% of the 3’UTRs and their associated PCTs were up-regulated on day 1, and in about 10% of the cases, they were both down-regulated.

On days 6 and 9, a somewhat smaller percentage of 3’UTR/PCT pairs were differentially expressed in the same direction (about 80% on both days), and about 40% or more of the pairs were down-regulated. The pattern of changes in expression of 3’UTR/CDS pairs was similar to the overall pattern of changes in expression of all PCT transcripts (paired with 3’UTRs and unpaired). When many of the paired PCTs were were up-regulated, all PCTs tended to be up-regulated, and vice-versa.

Changes in coexpression 3'UTR/PCT pairs were in line with expression profiles of all differentially expressed PCT identified in this study with 1,467 contigs up- and only 315 down-regulated after the 1st day. 3,648 and 6,543 after 6th day, 1,766 and 903 after 9th day were up- and down-regulated respectively (Figure X, C,D).

### MC-LR antagonizes expression profiles of some autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated PCTs (3'-UTRs with independent expression) 
As shown on figure X, we found that after MC-LR treatment not all expression profiles of pairs of 3'-UTR/PCT change in the same direction. In fact some of them showed the opposite direction in change of the expression. Day 1 showed the least number of this kind of contigs, but the proportions of the both groups with the opposite expression profiles remained stable throughout the experiment.

### Comparison between putative novel long non-coding and protein coding transcripts reveals differences in MFE, content of GC base pairs and length
To determine if the identified novel whitefish lncRNAs candidates differ from PCT in terms of a higher minimum free-energy (MFE) level than the protein coding transcripts, we used the RNAfold algorithm from the ViennaRNA package. Obtained free energy values of secondary structures were corrected for the length of the sequence (Figure 3 A). The mean length-corrected minimum free energy of the putative lncRNAs was significantly higher than that of the annotated protein coding transcripts (-0.237 kcal/mol/nt -+ 0.038758 vs. -0.289 kcal/mol/nt -+ 0.059464 kcal/mol/nt, respectively, t() = 46.652, p (lower) 0.001, 95 % [0.04963723, 0.05399174]. The mean length-corrected MFE content of the putative lncRNAs was 1.22 kcal/mol/nt higher than that of the protein-coding transcripts. Moreover the mean content of GC base pairs was higher in protein-coding transcripts (t() = 56,164, p (lower) 0.001, [0.06915164, 0.06448719]) (Figure 3 B). Finally length of the transcripts was found to be different in both groups (Figure 3 C). In summary, comparison analysis showed structural differences between putative lncRNAs and annotated PCT, validating our adopted methodology for discovery of lncRNAs in whitefish.

### MC-LR alters expression profiles of putative novel lncRNAs identified in this study
Using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor we investigated whether MC-LR induced changes in expression profiles of putative novel lncRNAs discovered in this study. Using a P adjusted (lower than) 0.001 and a 2-fold expression as a cutoff we identified 1739 and 2690 transcripts that were either up- or down-regulated between days of exposure, respectively. Figure X shows Venn diagrams with number of transcripts specific to the each time period, as well as those which overlap between days of exposure to MC-LR (Figure X; A,B). 31% of all up- and 34.8% of all down-regulated lncRNA transcripts were common between days 6 and 9 showing similar expression changes between these time periods. On the other hand, the similarities of common transcripts between day 1 and days 6 and 9 range from 0.23% to 5.31%. This pattern could be also observed in the protein coding transcripts identified in this study (Figure X; C,D).
