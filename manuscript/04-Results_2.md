### De novo transcriptome assembly allows identification (discovery) and classification of non-coding RNAs in whitefish exposed to MC-LR
Based on the procedure for identifying non-coding RNAs in non-model species first described by Harris et al., we first managed to separate protein coding transcripts (PCTs, xxx contigs) and further to discover various non-coding transcripts in whitefish (188,998 + known). Subsequent filtering steps allowed us to bin them into three non-overlapping groups. First group contained non-coding RNAs that showed homology to sequences deposited in Rfam database (the group of known non-coding transcripts, known NCTs with xxx contigs). Second group contained non-coding transcripts that had homology with non-coding 3' untranslated regions of mRNA sequences deposited in RefSeq database and had associated protein coding transcript (autonomous 3'UTR/PCT group with 104,024 contigs). The third group contained non-coding transcripts larger than 200 nucleotides with no homology to any tested database (putative novel long non-coding RNAs with 84,974 contigs). Our filtering process is summarized in the workflow diagram (Figure 1). 


### Expression of evolutionary conserved long non-coding RNAs change after MC-LR treatment
To obtain the list of evolutionary conserved long non-coding transcripts, transcripts left after removing protein coding transcripts (PCTs) were additionally checked for coding potential by SVM-based Coding Potential Calculator. Non-coding transcripts were further compared with sequences of transcripts deposited in the Rfam database. This produced the list of xxx contigs, which were counted and analyzed for differential expression (Figure 2a). Among 4,238 differentially expressed evolutionary conserved non-coding RNAs, there were 94 known, putatively conserved DE long non-coding RNAs identified (Figure 2b).


### Coexpression of autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated PCTs after MC-LR exposure
To investigate effects of MC-LR exposure on expression of non-coding contigs identified as autonomous 3'-UTRs, first we set them together with transcripts identified as coding transcripts (PCTs), that 3'-UTR is part of. Then, using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor we separately calculated differential expression of putative autonomous 3'-UTRs and associated PCTs. Finally we compared percentages of corresponding contigs, that both were up- or down-regulated, as well as those which expression profiles were contradictory (Figure 3 a). We found that, in the majority of cases, if an putative autonomous 3'-UTR was differentially expressed, the associated PCT was also differentially expressed in the same direction. The biggest shift in the direction of expression change was present after D1 where over 82% of transcripts pairs were upregulated and only 11% downregulated. This separates D1 from both D6 and D9 where up- and down-regulated pairs were present about in the same proportions (around 40%). Moreover we checked whether the pattern of changes in expression of coexpressed 3'-UTR/PCT pairs was reflected in the pattern of changes in expression of all PCT (paired with 3’UTRs and unpaired). We found that for D6 and D9 both expression patterns were similar but not identical, however up- and downregulated transcripts of D1 totaled 66 and 44% accordingly (Figure 3 c, d). 

Gene Ontology analysis of upregulated and downregulated pairs among all three time steps revealed that D6 and D9 showed very close similarities in GO terms (Figure 4 b,c). On the other hand, upregulated pairs of D1 were enriched in transcription regulator activity and DNA-binding transcription factor activity transcripts when compared with upregulated pairs of D6 and D9 (Figure 4 a). Moreover, downregulated pairs of D1 were depleted in transcripts involved in enzyme regulator activity processes when compared with downregulated pairs of D6 and D9.


### MC-LR antagonizes expression profiles of some autonomous 3'-UTRs and their associated PCTs
In addition, we found that MC-LR exposure changed expression of some 3'-UTRs in the opposite direction than PCTs of the same mRNA (Figure 3 a). Day 1 with the smallest proportion of this type of transcripts (7%), differed from day 6 and day 9 (19% and 16% accordingly), however the proportions of both groups with the opposite expression profiles remained on similar level throughout all days of exposure. GO terms analysis again showed similarities between day 6 and day 9, whereas day 1 was enriched in terms like 'signalling' or 'response to stimulus' in 3' UTRs down- and PCTs upregulated group. On the other hand days 6 and 9 were enriched in transcripts involved in 'cell', 'cell part', 'membrane' and 'membrane part' processes in 3' UTRs up- and PTCs downregulated group. ### Comparison between putative novel long non-coding and protein coding transcripts reveals differences in MFE, content of GC base pairs and length
To determine if the identified novel whitefish lncRNAs candidates differ from PCT in terms of a higher minimum free-energy (MFE) level than the protein coding transcripts, we used the RNAfold algorithm from the ViennaRNA package. Obtained free energy values of secondary structures were corrected for the length of the sequence (Figure 5 a). The mean length-corrected minimum free energy of the putative lncRNAs was significantly higher than that of the annotated protein coding transcripts (-0.237 kcal/mol/nt -+ 0.038758 vs. -0.289 kcal/mol/nt -+ 0.059464 kcal/mol/nt, respectively, t() = 46.652, p (lower) 0.001, 95 % [0.04963723, 0.05399174]. The mean length-corrected MFE content of the putative lncRNAs was 1.22 kcal/mol/nt higher than that of the protein-coding transcripts. Moreover the mean content of GC base pairs was higher in protein-coding transcripts (t() = 56,164, p (lower) 0.001, [0.06915164, 0.06448719]) (Figure 5 b). Finally length of the transcripts was found to be different in both groups (Figure 5 c). In summary, comparison analysis showed structural differences between putative lncRNAs and annotated PCT, validating our adopted methodology for discovery of lncRNAs in whitefish.


### MC-LR alters expression profiles of putative novel lncRNAs identified in this study
Using DESeq2 package of Bioconductor we investigated whether MC-LR induced changes in expression profiles of putative novel lncRNAs discovered in this study. Using a P adjusted (lower than) 0.001 and a 2-fold expression as a cutoff we identified 1739 and 2690 transcripts that were either up- or down-regulated between days of exposure, respectively. Figure 7 shows Venn diagrams with number of transcripts specific to the each time period, as well as those which overlap between days of exposure to MC-LR (Figure 7, A,B). 31% of all up- and 34.8% of all down-regulated lncRNA transcripts were common between days 6 and 9 showing similar expression changes between these time periods. On the other hand, the similarities of common transcripts between day 1 and days 6 and 9 range from 0.23% to 5.31%. This pattern could be also observed in the protein coding transcripts identified in this study (Figure X; C,D).
